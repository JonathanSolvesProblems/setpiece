# Setpiece charter

Second pass, 2026-11-05 (see ../direction-2.md). Laws 1, 7, 9, 11 and 16 below are replaced by these; the rest stand.

- **1b. Chalk is the text colour on cloth, with one exception: an actor's own line and tag are written in that actor's tape colour.** Tape colours are never used for anything that does not belong to that actor.
- **7b. Yellow marks what is selected or good to go, and nothing else:** the live tab, lens, shelf and camera, an asset that is on the set, a stage that has landed, the eyeline ball. Accent budget on the panel: one tab, one lens, one shelf, one camera, plus one edge per asset on the set.
- **9b. A thing that presses is a block with an edge; a thing that informs is plain chalk.** No control is a word with a rule under it.
- **11b. One filled chalk button per screen** (Snap frame, or Back to the table at full size, or Enter XR on the page). Selected blocks fill yellow; the record block fills red only while recording.
- **16b. Never pure white and never pure black.** The whitest thing is chalk #f6f7ef; the darkest is the cloth #1b1411, measured from a stage floor under spike tape.
- **19. The ground is black cloth, never grey and never glass.** No translucent panel, no blur.
- **20. Light means live.** Marks dim to 45% while their actor is in the air; nothing glows for decoration.
- **22. Everything on the slate starts as a maquette: bare basswood for vehicles and sets, carved wood for creatures.** Paint is the wearer's choice, in muted enamels. A scene from the library paints one piece at most. (Blind critic, 2026-11-05: toy-coloured models broke the set's one material language.)
- **21. A tape mark is a T, torn square, one per performer, flat on the floor, and out of the lens's picture.**


Laws every surface obeys: the headset scene, the panel, the 2D page, the submission images. Each has its reason. The machine-checkable part is `charter.json`.

1. **Chalk is the only text colour on slate; pencil and slate ink are the only text colours on paper.** Optic yellow and tally red are never text. On a slate, chalk is what writes; yellow is the ball and red is the tally (NOTES 1, 12, 13).
2. **Titles carry no full stop and no comma.** A question keeps its question mark. The slate's title is one word in chalk (NOTES 3).
3. **One loud line per screen, in Big Shoulders Stencil, at least four times the body size.** The stencil is otherwise used only for field labels and frame numbers, never for a sentence. GEDANKEN is loud and REGIE is small (NOTES 2, 3).
4. **A field is a stencil label on the left and a value on the right, on one line.** Never a label floating above a giant number. That is the slate's layout (NOTES 2).
5. **The clapper stripe appears exactly once per screen.** Under the loud line on the page; along the front edge of the stage plate in the headset. Never on a button, never around text, never tiled as a background (NOTES 4, 5).
6. **Nothing on the stage pretends to be the finished image.** Actors are wooden lay figures, the creature is a ball on a stand, the camera is a camera with a magazine. No faces, no costumes, no creature model (NOTES 7, 12).
7. **Optic yellow marks three things and nothing else:** the eyeline ball, the selected lens, and a stage state that is good to go. Nothing decorative is yellow (NOTES 12).
8. **Tally red appears only while something records:** the tally on the camera and the snap flash. Errors are chalk on slate under a stencil label; red is not an error colour (NOTES 13).
9. **The monitor is the only bordered object.** No cards, no outlined boxes. Groups are separated by space and one chalk rule (design code 4).
10. **Chalk haze sits on every slate surface at low opacity**, never over text and never inside the viewfinder image (NOTES 1).
11. **One filled button per screen**, the primary action (Snap frame in the headset, Enter XR on the page). Every other control is a chalk word with a two-pixel chalk rule under it when active.
12. **Numbers sit in fixed columns with fixed precision:** lenses as integers with mm, heights and distances to one decimal in m, angles as integers in deg. A missing value is a word, never a dash (design code 5).
13. **Motion is one detented beat per action.** The clapper closes on snap in 120 ms, holds, opens in 240 ms. Nothing animates on exit. Reduced motion removes the clapper and keeps the flash.
14. **Illustrations are chalk on slate in one hand**, drawn directly on the slate: no disc, no plate, no border behind them. On paper they are redrawn in pencil, never inverted (NOTES 9, 11).
15. **Copy uses the camera department's words:** shot, take, lens, eyeline, mark, slate, board, plate. Never immersive, seamless, experience, intuitive, powerful, next-generation.
16. **Never pure white and never pure black.** The whitest thing is chalk #f6f7ef; the darkest is the slate's shadow #1e232b (NOTES 1, 9).
17. **The page signs itself in the footer** with a sentence about the product and a link to jonathansolvesproblems.com, in the muted chalk, after any platform line.
18. **Wood is reserved for the figures.** No wood tones on surfaces, buttons or type; the lay figures are the only warm objects on the slate (NOTES 7).
